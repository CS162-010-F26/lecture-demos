Problem: Write a function that can compute the difference in days between
two dates (Date 1 and Date 2).

Leap years make this difficult.

Every four years is a leap year, except for years that are divisible by
100 but NOT 400.

Different months have different numbers of days in them.



To design this function using top-down design:

1. Compute the number of days to get from Date 1 to the beginning of the next
     year after the one that Date 1 is in. 
2. For each year in between Date 1 and Date 2 (inclusive), figure out how
     many days are in that year and add it to our running total
3. Compute the number of days to get from Jan 1 of the year that Date 2 is in,
     to Date 2 itself

Exception: If Date 1 and Date 2 are in the same year:

1. Compute the number of days between the dates, given that they're in the
     same year


Recognize: If we can compute the difference between two dates that are in
    the same year, we can solve a lot of these smaller problems.

To compute the difference between two dates (Date 1 and Date 2) that are in
the same year:

1. Compute the number of days to get from Date 1 to the beginning of the
     next month after the one that Date 1 is in (subtract # of days in
     month from the day of the month of Date 1, then add 1 to the result)
2. For each month in between Date 1 and Date 2 (inclusive), figure out how
     many days are in that month and add it to our running total
3. Add the day of the month of Date 2, minus 1, to our running total


Another smaller component: Figure out if a year is a leap year.

1. If year is divisible by 100 but not 400:
	return False
2. Else if the year is divisible by 4:
	return True
3. Else return False





We like to design things top-down. However, we often prefer to implement
things bottom-up.

What's the advantage to implementing bottom-up? The high-level functions
depend on the low-level ones, so the high-level function will never work
properly until the low-level ones all, also work properly.
On the other hand, the low-level functions can theoretically work properly
independently of the high-level functions, in isolation.

(The low-level functions do not depend on anything).

The fact that the low-level functions work right away, they can also be
tested right away.
